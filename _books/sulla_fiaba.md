_On Folktales_ is a collection of essays wrote by Calvino on how people have approached collecting and writing down folktales from the 19th century. The book gave me a set of useful tools for approaching folktales more attentively. In particular, Calvino helped me look for recurring structures and character types, while also paying attention to the editorial choices that shape how these stories reach the reader. It did not make me see every tale as part of a single rigid system, but rather showed me some of the underlying properties I might otherwise have overlooked.

The style is more academic than Calvino's novels, but it remains clear. I found the essays interesting and, above all, valuable as preparation for reading [_Italian Folktales_](/books/fiabe_italiane/): they gave me a framework I can now use without prescribing what I should find.

I would not recommend _On Folktales_ universally. It is a specialized book, best suited to readers who are already interested in folktales and want to examine them with greater awareness.
