Whenever I visit a new country, I buy a copy of _The Little Prince_ in the local language. My curiosity about languages started the collection: over time, it became both a ritual and an unusual kind of souvenir. Each edition brings back where I found it and what that journey meant. Its emotional value is far greater than its material one.

As a child, I was most captivated by the fox. “Taming” helped me understand how people become important to one another, although I understood that bond in the slightly self-centred way children often do: primarily through what loving and being loved gave me.

Rereading the book as an adult, I found myself strongly drawn instead to the king and the businessman. What once seemed merely funny now feels unsettling. The businessman reduces value to counting and ownership; the king reduces relationships to control. Both resemble the pressures we absorb as adults: the insistence that possessions, productivity, status, authority and power are what matter because they can be measured, displayed or wielded.

I would recommend _The Little Prince_ to anyone, at any age, and then recommend reading it again later. The story remains the same, but it reveals something different as we ourselves change.
