I first read _Sostiene Pereira_ in high school and loved it. Years later, I remembered that affection and the general plot more clearly than the details. Rereading confirmed why I had loved it, and once again I read it very quickly. It is short, but above all exceptionally clear and effortless to read.

Pereira repeatedly insists that he is not political, even as he lives under an authoritarian regime. What stayed with me most was his gradual awakening to that reality. His relationship with Monteiro Rossi drives the change. The articles Rossi submits are politically unpublishable, yet Pereira keeps them. Those decisions suggest that his conscience is moving before he can admit it: he is drawn to the ideas in the articles and unwilling to destroy them.

For me, the novel's most meaningful insight is that relationships with people very different from us can change how we see the world and reveal what we have learned to accept. Pereira's final choice completes a moral awakening that necessarily becomes a political act, making his old insistence on being apolitical impossible to maintain.

Tabucchi's prose is crisp, clear and highly visual: I could see the scenes as I read them. The recurring _sostiene Pereira_ made the novel feel to me like a deposition, perhaps testimony given after the events. For me, the formula gives the story an unusual clarity.

I would recommend this novel universally. It is simply very well written, combining remarkable ease of reading with genuine moral depth.
