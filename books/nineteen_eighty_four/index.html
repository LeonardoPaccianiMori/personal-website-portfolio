1984

Nineteen Eighty-Four

George Orwell · 1949

Started March 26, 2026 Finished April 18, 2026
novel dystopian political-fiction totalitarianism

My note

I first read 1984 at school many years ago. I returned to it alongside Fahrenheit 451, hoping that these two novels might offer help in interpreting and understanding our current historical period, which is not a stretch to define dystopian.

Newspeak and the reasoning behind it made a strong impression on me. The idea that language can be deliberately narrowed to restrict what people are capable of expressing (and eventually thinking) feels extraordinarily powerful. I was also struck by the way Winston describes the room above Mr Charrington’s shop and how he feels when he’s there: a precious, fragile private space in which he feels genuinely free.

This rereading made me think deeply about the relationship between language, surveillance, and control. I repeatedly found myself thinking about social media and the ways in which today’s platforms can shape attention, expression, and ultimately thought itself, and how the fact that basically everyone today carries a camera with them at all times makes our world not that different from having telescreens watching and following us everywhere we go. Orwell’s world is still (luckily!) extreme, but unfortunately the mechanisms he explores do not feel remote.

I loved the novel without reservation: the story, its message, and Orwell’s writing all worked for me. I will certainly read it again, and I would recommend it to anyone seeking a sharper perspective on some of the forces shaping our own time.
